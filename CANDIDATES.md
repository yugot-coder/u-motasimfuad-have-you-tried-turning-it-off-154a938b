# Candidates — Have You Tried Turning It Off?

Competition: `u-motasimfuad-have-you-tried-turning-it-off-154a938b`
Gate: one line, plain text, 1–199 characters, written to `entry.txt`.
Judge (pairwise, funnier line wins): on-topic — a funny single-line reply
from a hopeless tech support agent to a desperate customer; the twist is
unexpected, the agent's cheerful uselessness is where the humour comes
from, and the customer is never the butt of it; sounds like one line a real
support agent would actually say, with the customer's desperation felt
rather than explained; tight — every word earns its place, clear on a
first read; fresh — if it leans on the classic "turn it off and on again",
it twists it. Exact ties on judge win rate go to the smaller text (fewer
bytes), then to the earlier submission — so a short, early entry is best.

## Chosen entry — #4

> Good news: I turned it off and on again. Bad news: that was my computer, not yours.

Why this one wins the brief:

- **Twists the classic instead of repeating it.** The field will be flooded
  with straight "have you tried turning it off and on again" lines; this one
  takes the ritual and turns it back on the agent himself.
- **The humour is the agent's cheerful uselessness.** He did the one thing
  support is famous for — on the wrong computer — and reports it as good
  news. The customer is never the butt of the joke.
- **Sounds like a real agent line.** The good-news/bad-news frame is exactly
  how support agents soften bad news on a call, and the customer's
  desperation is felt in the gap between the two sentences.
- **Turn at the last word.** The punchline lands on "not yours" — the laugh
  arrives exactly where the brief wants it.
- **Economy.** 83 characters, 83 bytes — short, which also helps the
  smaller-text tie-break. No throat-clearing, no second sentence.

Rejected notes: #1/#6/#7 lean on the classic without a strong enough twist
and risk reading as the stock line the brief warns against; #2 is charming
but its joke is softer and the twist arrives mid-line; #3 is funny but
longer and its "louder" escalation is a second joke competing with the
first; #5 is warm but the laugh is about the agent's own hold time, not the
support ritual; #8 is sweet but the punchline ("until it feels loved") is
gentle rather than surprising; #9 is close but "Your turn" makes the
customer do the work, softening the agent's uselessness; #10 is clever but
"faith in technology" is abstract where #4 stays concrete.
